<Aside type="note" title="Pre-release">
Everything Nodearr set out to do for its first version works, and has been run between machines on different home networks with no port forwarded. It has not yet been lived with for weeks. See [what is still open](https://github.com/Nodearr/Nodearr/blob/main/docs/plans/v1-implementation-plan.md#what-is-still-open).
</Aside>

When your Sonarr wants an episode a friend has, it finds it in Nodearr, grabs it, and imports it like any other download. The file comes straight from your friend's machine to yours, and is checked against its hash before your Arr app sees it.

Nobody else is involved. There is no account, no server of ours that sees your library, and no relay that carries your files. Each of you runs Nodearr on your own machine, decides what the others may see, and can end a friendship at any time.

## What it works with

| App | What Nodearr does with it |
| --- | --- |
| **Sonarr** | Shares whole series, seasons or single episodes. Finds episodes by TVDB, TMDb or IMDb ID, by scene numbering, and by air date for daily shows. |
| **Radarr** | Finds movies by TMDb or IMDb ID. |
| **Lidarr** | Shares whole artists or single albums, and moves an album as a folder of tracks. |
| **Whisparr v3 and v2** | Finds films by ID, and scenes by studio, release date, title and code, as Whisparr searches for them. |
| **Prowlarr** | Adds Nodearr as an indexer, so you can search it there with your others. |

It runs where your Arr apps run: Windows, Linux, macOS, and Docker for amd64 and arm64. Most of it is built from [Sonarr](https://github.com/Sonarr/Sonarr), so it looks and works the way you expect.
